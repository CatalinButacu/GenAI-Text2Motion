\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Text-to-motion synthesis has reached high fidelity on the HumanML3D benchmark, but the most accurate systems attend bidirectionally over the whole sequence, which rules out real-time, incremental generation. This thesis studies the mechanism needed for streaming: a generator that is causal and whose per-step memory does not grow with the length of the motion. All results are measured on the standard HumanML3D-263 representation with the unmodified evaluator of Guo et al.~\cite{guo2022humanml3d}, so that every number is comparable with published work.

The thesis makes three contributions. First, a \emph{Grouped-FSQ motion tokenizer}, which combines finite scalar quantization~\cite{mentzer2023fsq} with a grouped-residual structure. Against a strong residual vector-quantization baseline that shares its encoder, decoder, data, seed and budget, it has lower reconstruction FID at all six matched bit-rate cells and reaches a reconstruction FID of $0.0170$ with no codebook parameters.

Second, a \emph{token-autoregressive state-space generator}: a discrete, causal, next-token Mamba (S6) model for text-to-motion, compared against a causal transformer with nearly the same parameter count ($15.55$M and $15.30$M) that differs only in the sequence mixer. Both are selected on validation and scored once on the test split ($4648$ segments, 20 repetitions). The text match is equal: R@1 is $0.505$ against $0.500$, and a paired test finds no difference (McNemar $p = 0.42$). The transformer has the lower FID ($0.085$ against $0.110$; paired difference $-0.036$, $95\%$ interval $[-0.065, -0.011]$), and the state-space model has more foot sliding. The comparison rests on a single seed.

Third, a \emph{bounded-memory streaming system}. The recurrent state of the state-space generator is constant at $0.73$~MB at every horizon, while the transformer's key--value cache grows linearly to $168$~MB at $8192$ tokens. We measure the tokenizer decoder's receptive field, show that a fixed lookahead of four latent tokens ($0.8$~s) makes streamed decoding identical to whole-sequence decoding, and integrate the pipeline into an interactive viewer. A test on streams seven times longer than the training clips shows the limit of the result: the state keeps its size, but the motion stays good only when the generator is restarted from a short window of recent tokens.

We do not claim state-of-the-art absolute quality. The claims concern the mechanism, under a controlled comparison, at a scale of about $15$M parameters that a single 4~GB GPU can train.
