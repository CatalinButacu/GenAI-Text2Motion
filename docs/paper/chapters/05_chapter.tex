\chapter{Contribution II: Token-Autoregressive State-Space Generator}
\label{chap:generator}

\section{Hypothesis: Bounded State Without Quality Loss}
\label{sec:gen_hypothesis}

A state-space layer summarises the whole past in a state of fixed size, whereas causal attention can look up any past position exactly. One would expect the fixed state to cost quality. This chapter tests that expectation.

\begin{hyp}
\label{hyp:parity}
With tokenizer, data, seed, parameter count, training budget and sampling settings held equal, a token-autoregressive generator whose sequence mixer is a selective state-space layer reaches generation quality on HumanML3D-263 at least equal to that of the same generator with causal self-attention.
\end{hyp}

The memory side of the comparison, that the state-space generator carries $O(1)$ state where the transformer carries $O(L)$, follows from the recurrences and is measured in \chapref{chap:streaming}.

\section{Method: The Token-Autoregressive S6 Generator}
\label{sec:gen_method}

\paragraph{Factorisation.} The frozen tokenizer of \chapref{chap:tokenizer} turns a motion into $S$ latent steps, each with $Q = 8$ codes of vocabulary $V = 1024$. Write $z_{t,q}$ for code $q$ of step $t$ and $c$ for the caption. The generator models
\begin{equation}
p_\theta(z \mid c) = \prod_{t=1}^{S} \prod_{q=1}^{Q} p_\theta\bigl(z_{t,q} \mid z_{<t},\, c\bigr),
\label{eq:factorisation}
\end{equation}
so the $Q$ codes of one step are predicted in parallel from the same hidden state, and steps are predicted in order. An additional END symbol at the step after the last one lets the model decide the length of the motion.

\paragraph{Inputs.} The caption is encoded by the CLIP ViT-B/32 text encoder~\cite{radford2021clip}. Its pooled, projected 512-dimensional vector is mapped linearly to the model width and placed at the start of the sequence as a prefix token. This is the conditioning used by T2M-GPT and MoMask, and it needs no cross-attention, so both backbones can share it. Each motion step is embedded as the sum of $Q$ learned embeddings, one per codebook.

\paragraph{Backbone.} The sequence passes through $N$ pre-norm residual blocks,
\begin{equation}
h \leftarrow h + \operatorname{Mixer}\bigl(\operatorname{RMSNorm}(h)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the mixer is causal. Its two variants are described below. $Q$ linear heads map the final hidden state of each position to logits over the next step's codes.

\paragraph{The S6 mixer.} The input of width $d$ is projected to width $d_{\mathrm{in}} = 2d$, passed through a depthwise causal convolution of kernel 4 and a SiLU nonlinearity, and then through the selective recurrence. For each of the $d_{\mathrm{in}}$ channels there is a state of dimension $n = 16$:
\begin{equation}
s_t = e^{\Delta_t A} \odot s_{t-1} + \Delta_t\, B_t\, u_t, \qquad y_t = C_t^{\top} s_t + D\, u_t ,
\label{eq:s6}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
A = -\exp(a_{\log}) < 0, \qquad \Delta_t = \operatorname{softplus}\bigl(W_\Delta\, u_t + b_\Delta\bigr) > 0,
\label{eq:s6_param}
\end{equation}
and $B_t$, $C_t$ linear functions of $u_t$. The output is gated by a SiLU branch and projected back to width $d$. For training we use the compiled selective-scan kernel of Mamba~\cite{gu2023mamba}; a reference implementation in plain PyTorch gives the same outputs.

Every factor $e^{\Delta_t A}$ in \eqref{eq:s6} lies in $(0, 1)$ by \eqref{eq:s6_param}. The recurrence is therefore contractive by construction: gradients through time cannot grow, and the rate at which they decay is exactly what the input controls. A small $\Delta_t$ keeps the state; a large $\Delta_t$ overwrites it.

For training, \eqref{eq:s6} is evaluated for all positions at once with the parallel scan of the kernel. For inference, one step of \eqref{eq:s6} is applied to the stored state. A test asserts that the two give the same outputs, so the model that is evaluated is the model that streams.

\paragraph{Guidance and sampling.} During training the caption is replaced by a null condition with probability $0.1$, so one network learns both $p_\theta(z \mid c)$ and $p_\theta(z)$. At inference the logits are combined by classifier-free guidance~\cite{ho2022cfg},
\begin{equation}
\ell = \ell_{\varnothing} + s\,(\ell_{c} - \ell_{\varnothing}),
\label{eq:cfg}
\end{equation}
with scale $s$, and tokens are drawn by nucleus sampling~\cite{holtzman2020nucleus} at temperature $\tau$. We do not use beam search. Text-to-motion is one-to-many, and a search for the most likely sequence reduces diversity; a beam of width $b$ would also multiply the recurrent state by $b$.

\paragraph{Objective.} The main term is the cross-entropy of \eqref{eq:factorisation} under teacher forcing. Token cross-entropy treats all wrong codes as equally wrong, although some decode to nearly the same motion. We therefore add reconstruction terms in feature space. The predicted distribution of each code is turned into an expected code vector, the expected vectors are composed as in \eqref{eq:residual} and passed through the frozen tokenizer decoder, and the decoded features $\hat{x}$ are compared with the ground truth $x$ by an $L_1$ loss on each block of \tabref{tab:layout263}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}} + \sum_{k} w_k \,\bigl\lVert \hat{x}^{(k)} - x^{(k)} \bigr\rVert_1 , \qquad k \in \{\text{root}, \text{positions}, \text{rotations}, \text{velocities}, \text{contacts}\},
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with weights $(0.3, 0.5, 0.5, 0.3, 0.1)$.

\paragraph{Optimisation.} We use AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} with learning rate $2 \cdot 10^{-4}$, linear warm-up over 1000 steps and cosine decay to $1\%$ of the peak over the whole run. Weight decay is $0.01$ on weight matrices; biases, normalisation gains and the state-space parameters $a_{\log}$ and $D$ are exempt, since decaying $a_{\log}$ would shorten the memory of the recurrence. Gradients are clipped to norm $1.0$. The inputs under teacher forcing are corrupted while the targets stay clean: each latent step keeps its $Q$ input codes with probability $0.5$ and otherwise has all $Q$ codes replaced by random codes. Dropout is $0.1$. An exponential moving average of the weights with decay $0.999$ is what is evaluated. The last layer, final normalisation and projection of the CLIP text encoder are fine-tuned at learning rate $10^{-5}$; the rest of it is frozen.

\section{Controlled Protocol: The Parameter-Matched Transformer Twin}
\label{sec:gen_twin}

The baseline is the same generator with the S6 mixer replaced by causal multi-head self-attention followed by a feed-forward layer~\cite{vaswani2017attention}, with learned absolute position embeddings. This is the decoder-only design of T2M-GPT~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt}. We keep it standard on purpose: a specially tuned baseline would make the comparison hard to interpret.

An S6 block has no separate feed-forward layer, because its expansion projections play that role, so it has roughly half the parameters of a transformer block. The layer counts are set so that the totals match.

\section{Training and Checkpoint Selection}
\label{sec:gen_training}

Both generators are trained from random initialisation on the captioned HumanML3D training split only; there is no pretraining stage. Each training clip is used together with its mirrored copy. A clip longer than 196 frames is cropped at a random position, and one of its captions is drawn at random, in each epoch.

\paragraph{Budget and stop rule.} The learning-rate schedule is set for 60 epochs. After each epoch, the token cross-entropy on the validation split is computed without input noise. Training stops when six consecutive epochs give no lower validation cross-entropy. The transformer did not reach this point and trained for all 60 epochs. The state-space model stopped at epoch 41.

\paragraph{Selection.} Every second epoch, FID and R@1 are computed on 600 validation clips. The selection rule was fixed before any test run: among the evaluated epochs whose validation R@1 is not more than $0.02$ below a reference value from an earlier run of the same size ($0.490$ for the transformer, $0.482$ for the state-space model), the epoch with the lowest validation FID is kept. This selected epoch 60 for the transformer (validation FID $0.149$, R@1 $0.498$) and epoch 24 for the state-space model (validation FID $0.153$, R@1 $0.507$). The test split was used once, after this selection.

The paired tests agree with \tabref{tab:twin_results}. The FID difference favours the transformer, and its interval excludes zero. The text match does not differ: neither the rank-1 rate nor the distance to the caption gives a significant difference. Both generators slide their feet more than real motion, by about $50\%$ for the transformer and $60\%$ for the state-space model, and the difference between them is significant. Both are slightly smoother than real motion; the state-space model is nearer to the real acceleration and jerk.

\paragraph{Validation split.} The same paired analysis on the 765 validation clips gives an FID difference of $-0.006$ with interval $[-0.102, +0.092]$ and a rank-1 rate of $0.472$ against $0.500$ (McNemar $p = 0.18$). The validation split is about one sixth of the test population, and its interval is about four times wider; it does not resolve the FID difference that the test split shows.

\hypref{hyp:parity} asks for quality at least equal to the transformer's. It is supported for text--motion matching and not supported for FID and foot sliding: the fixed-size state did not cost text match, but it cost a small and significant amount of distribution quality.

Two qualifications apply to the cited rows. They come from different training runs and larger models, and we make no claim relative to them. MoMask remains far ahead in absolute quality and is bidirectional.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:gen_threats}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single seed.} The comparison is one training run per model. The evaluation is averaged over 20 repetitions, which controls sampling noise but not the variation between training runs.
\item \textbf{Different stop epochs.} Both runs had the same schedule and the same stop rule, but the transformer trained for 60 epochs and was still improving, while the state-space model stopped at epoch 41 and was selected at epoch 24. A longer budget could change the FID difference in either direction.
\item \textbf{Scale.} The result is established at 15M parameters. \secref{sec:disc_data} argues that this data set does not support much larger models, but we have not measured a matched pair at another scale.
\item \textbf{Validation sample.} Selection uses 600 validation clips. The validation split did not show the FID difference that the test split shows (\secref{sec:gen_results}), so a selection rule on validation FID has limited power at this size.
\item \textbf{Baseline design.} The transformer uses learned absolute positions and no architectural refinements. A more elaborate transformer might do better; we chose the standard form so that the baseline is recognisable.
\end{itemize}
