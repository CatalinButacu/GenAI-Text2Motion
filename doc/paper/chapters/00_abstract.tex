\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Text-to-motion synthesis has reached high fidelity on the HumanML3D benchmark, but the most accurate systems attend bidirectionally over the whole sequence, which rules out real-time, incremental generation. This thesis studies the mechanism needed for streaming: a generator that is causal and whose per-step memory does not grow with the length of the motion. All results are measured on the standard HumanML3D-263 representation with the unmodified evaluator of Guo et al.~\cite{guo2022humanml3d}, so that every number is comparable with published work.

The thesis makes three contributions. First, a \emph{Grouped-FSQ motion tokenizer}, which combines finite scalar quantization~\cite{mentzer2023fsq} with a grouped-residual structure. Against a strong residual vector-quantization baseline that shares its encoder, decoder, data, seed and budget, it has lower reconstruction FID at all six matched bit-rate cells and reaches a reconstruction FID of $0.0170$ with no codebook parameters.

Second, a \emph{token-autoregressive state-space generator}: a discrete, causal, next-token Mamba (S6) model for text-to-motion, compared against a parameter-matched causal transformer that differs only in the sequence mixer. At a converged 60-epoch budget on the full test split ($2189$ clips, 20 repetitions), the state-space model is ahead of the transformer in point estimates on every metric: FID $0.121$ against $0.132$, R@1 $0.418$ against $0.395$, and likewise on multimodal distance and diversity. The R-precision lead exceeds the evaluation's repetition noise; the FID difference lies within its bootstrap interval, so on FID the two are on par. A longer-training check and a loss ablation, each applied to both models as a matched pair, do not change this ordering. The comparison rests on a single seed.

Third, a \emph{bounded-memory streaming system}. The recurrent state of the state-space generator is constant at $2.68$~MB at every horizon, while the transformer's key--value cache grows linearly to $605$~MB at $8192$ tokens. We measure the tokenizer decoder's receptive field, show that a fixed lookahead of four latent tokens ($0.8$~s) makes streamed decoding identical to whole-sequence decoding, and integrate the pipeline into an interactive viewer. A test on streams four times longer than the training clips shows the limit of the result: the state keeps its size, but the motion stays good only when the generator is restarted from a short window of recent tokens.

We do not claim state-of-the-art absolute quality. The claims concern the mechanism, under a controlled comparison, at a $34$M-parameter scale that a single 4~GB GPU can train.
