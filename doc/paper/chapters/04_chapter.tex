\label{chap:tokenizer}

\section{Problem: Codebook Fragility in Vector Quantization}
\label{sec:tok_problem}

The quality of a token-based generator is bounded by its tokenizer: the generator cannot produce motion that the decoder cannot reconstruct. In vector quantization the bottleneck is a learned codebook, and \secref{sec:bg_tokenization} recalled that it works only with additional machinery. An exponential moving average replaces the gradient update of the codes, a commitment term keeps the encoder close to them, and codes that fall out of use are periodically reinitialised. Each of these adds hyperparameters, and the codebook itself adds parameters.

The question of this chapter is whether a quantizer with no learned codebook can do better than a well-tuned residual vector quantizer on the standard motion representation, at the same bit-rate and with everything else held equal.

\section{Method: Grouped Finite Scalar Quantization}
\label{sec:tok_method}

\paragraph{Encoder and decoder.} A 1D convolutional encoder maps $T$ frames of dimension 263 to $T/4$ latent vectors of width 512, using two stride-2 stages followed by three residual blocks at the latent rate. At 20 frames per second the latent rate is 5~Hz. The decoder mirrors the encoder with transposed convolutions.

\paragraph{Finite scalar quantization.} FSQ~\cite{mentzer2023fsq} quantizes each channel of a low-dimensional projection independently. A channel with $L$ levels is bounded and rounded:
\begin{equation}
f(z) = \frac{L-1}{2}\tanh(z), \qquad \hat{z} = z + \operatorname{sg}\bigl(\operatorname{round}(f(z)) - z\bigr),
\label{eq:fsq}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}$ is the stop-gradient operator. In the forward pass $\hat{z} = \operatorname{round}(f(z))$; in the backward pass the gradient of $\hat{z}$ with respect to $z$ is the identity, which is the straight-through estimator. A group of channels with levels $(L_1, \dots, L_G)$ defines an implicit codebook of
\begin{equation}
V = \prod_{g=1}^{G} L_g
\end{equation}
entries, and a code index is the mixed-radix number formed by the $G$ rounded values. No entry of this codebook is a parameter.

\paragraph{Grouped-residual structure.} One group does not carry enough information for a 263-dimensional frame at 5~Hz. We therefore apply $Q$ such quantizers in sequence. With $r_1$ the encoder output,
\begin{equation}
\hat{z}_i = \operatorname{FSQ}_i(r_i), \qquad r_{i+1} = r_i - \hat{z}_i, \qquad \hat{z} = \sum_{i=1}^{Q} \hat{z}_i ,
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
so each quantizer encodes what the previous ones missed. A latent step is thus described by $Q$ integer codes, and the bit-rate per step is
\begin{equation}
R = Q \sum_{g=1}^{G} \log_2 L_g = Q \log_2 V .
\end{equation}
The configuration used by the generator has $Q = 8$ and levels $(8, 8, 4, 4)$, so $V = 1024$ and $R = 80$ bits per latent step. During training, the number of active quantizers is randomly truncated with probability $0.2$, which makes the first codes carry the coarse structure.

FSQ has been applied to motion before~\cite{lu2024scamo,wang2025stmsq}, and residual quantization is standard~\cite{guo2024momask}. What is evaluated here is their combination on HumanML3D-263 against a matched residual VQ.

\section{Controlled Baseline: A Strong Residual VQ}
\label{sec:tok_baseline}

The baseline is a residual vector quantizer that shares the encoder and decoder of \secref{sec:tok_method}, so that the comparison isolates the quantizer. It uses the recipe that the literature has shown to be necessary~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt,guo2024momask}: codebook updates by exponential moving average with decay $0.99$, commitment weight $0.02$, reinitialisation of dead codes, quantizer dropout $0.2$ and an auxiliary velocity loss of weight $0.1$.

Both tokenizers are trained for 500 epochs with the same data, seed and budget. We vary the number of codes per step, $Q \in \{4, 6, 8\}$, and the vocabulary per code. Cells with equal $Q$ and equal vocabulary have equal bit-rate.

Let $D_{\mathrm{FSQ}}(R)$ and $D_{\mathrm{RVQ}}(R)$ be the reconstruction FID at bit-rate $R$. At vocabulary 512 the three matched pairs are $0.0612 < 0.0626$, $0.0305 < 0.0342$ and $0.0196 < 0.0277$. At vocabulary 1024 they are $0.0527 < 0.0558$, $0.0283 < 0.0310$ and $0.0170 < 0.0221$. Hence
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{FSQ}}(R) < D_{\mathrm{RVQ}}(R) \quad \text{at all six matched rates.}
\end{equation}
Because the inequality holds at equal vocabulary, the gain cannot be attributed to a larger codebook. The margin grows with the bit-rate: it is about $2\%$ at four codes of 512 and about $23\%$ at eight codes of 1024.

The residual VQ carries between $1.6$M and $3$M codebook parameters depending on the cell; Grouped-FSQ carries none.

For context, T2M-GPT reports $0.071$ for its VQ tokenizer~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt} and MoMask reports $0.019$ for its residual VQ~\cite{guo2024momask}. Our best cell, $0.0170$, is below both. We do not read this as beating a best-tuned residual VQ in general: our own residual VQ reaches $0.0221$, so it is weaker than MoMask's, and the claim of this section is the controlled inequality above.

\section{Results: Codebook Health}
\label{sec:tok_health}

Reconstruction error does not show how a codebook is used. A tokenizer can reconstruct well while most of its codes are idle. We report two standard diagnostics at the end of training. With $p$ the empirical distribution over the $V$ codes of one quantizer, the \emph{perplexity} is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PPL} = \exp\Bigl(-\sum_{k=1}^{V} p_k \ln p_k\Bigr),
\end{equation}
which equals $V$ under uniform use and $1$ under collapse onto a single code. The \emph{usage} is the fraction of codes selected at least once.

At equal vocabulary, FSQ has higher usage than the residual VQ in all six matched pairs, and higher perplexity in five of them; the exception is eight codes of 512, where the two are within one percent (340 against 343). The reconstruction advantage of \secref{sec:tok_rd} is therefore not obtained by concentrating on fewer codes.

\section{Results: Downstream Generation Quality}
\label{sec:tok_downstream}

\paragraph{Status of these results.} The generation numbers in this section come from the earlier evaluation protocol of \chapref{chap:generator}. The reconstruction numbers of the previous sections do not depend on that protocol. Matched runs with the corrected protocol will replace the generation numbers.

A lower reconstruction FID is useful only if a generator trained on the tokens is also better. We trained the transformer generator of \chapref{chap:generator} on the tokens of the Grouped-FSQ $8 \times 1024$ tokenizer and on those of the residual VQ $8 \times 1024$ tokenizer, with the protocol of \chapref{chap:generator}, and scored both on the full test split.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Test FID of a generator trained on each token stream, at two epoch budgets.}
\label{tab:tok_downstream}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Token stream & 18 epochs & 60 epochs \\
\midrule
Residual VQ $8 \times 1024$ & 0.832 & 0.294 \\
Grouped-FSQ $8 \times 1024$ & 3.30 & \textbf{0.132} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

At 18 epochs the residual VQ tokens give the better generator. At 60 epochs the order is reversed: FID $0.132$ for FSQ tokens against $0.294$ for residual VQ tokens. The $95\%$ bootstrap intervals over test clips, $[0.127, 0.230]$ and $[0.251, 0.428]$, do not overlap. R@1 is not distinguishable between the two ($0.395$ against $0.392$). The 18-epoch reading reflected an undertrained generator. The FSQ token stream is harder to fit early and better once fitted.

With this result, Grouped-FSQ leads the residual VQ on all three axes we measured: reconstruction, codebook usage and downstream generation.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:tok_threats}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single seed.} Every cell of Tables~\ref{tab:tok_matrix}--\ref{tab:tok_downstream} is one run. The smallest margins, at four codes per step, could change sign under a different seed. The margins at eight codes per step are large relative to any variation we observed between cells.
\item \textbf{Baseline strength.} Our residual VQ ($0.0221$) is weaker than the published MoMask tokenizer ($0.019$), which was trained with more compute. The comparison is controlled; it is not a comparison against the best residual VQ ever trained.
\item \textbf{Overfitting.} We compared reconstruction FID on train, validation and test splits at equal clip counts. The train--test gap is non-positive in 13 of the 15 cells, and both positive gaps belong to residual VQ cells, so the FSQ advantage is not memorisation of the training split.
\item \textbf{Non-causal decoder.} The decoder uses symmetric convolutions and therefore needs a short lookahead when streaming. \chapref{chap:streaming} measures this and its cost.
\end{itemize}
