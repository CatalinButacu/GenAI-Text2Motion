\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis asked what it costs to make a text-to-motion generator streamable: causal, and with a memory that does not grow with the length of the motion. It built the pieces such a system needs, a tokenizer, a generator and a decoding pipeline, and measured each against a controlled alternative.

The expectation we set out to test was that a fixed-size state would cost quality relative to attention. In our controlled comparison it cost a small amount: the text match was equal, and FID and foot sliding were slightly worse. One seed at one scale cannot support a general statement about state-space models of motion. We conclude that the bounded-state design is a viable basis for streaming text-to-motion: at this scale, its constant memory costs no text match and a small, measured part of the distribution quality.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc_future}

In order of what would most strengthen or extend the results:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{More seeds.} Repeating the matched pair with two or three further seeds would turn the ordering of \tabref{tab:twin_results} from an observation into an estimate with a spread.
\item \textbf{More distinct motion.} \secref{sec:disc_data} identifies data, not epochs or parameters, as the binding constraint. The cheapest step is to use the $40.6$ hours of our AMASS conversion, which the final generators do not use, for unconditional pretraining.
\item \textbf{A causal tokenizer decoder.} A decoder without right context would remove the $0.8$~s lookahead of \secref{sec:stream_decoder}. Its effect on reconstruction FID would have to be measured against \tabref{tab:tok_matrix}.
\item \textbf{Training for long streams.} The full recurrent state fails after the trained length (\tabref{tab:long_stream}). Training on sequences longer than ten seconds, for example on concatenated clips with a caption for each part, is what would turn the constant memory of the state-space model into a practical advantage.
\item \textbf{A fused step and a parallel rebuild.} The step of the state-space layer runs in plain PyTorch (\secref{sec:stream_state}), and a reset rebuilds the state one token at a time (\secref{sec:stream_long}). A fused step kernel and a rebuild with the parallel scan would lower the inference cost of the state-space generator.
\item \textbf{Contact-aware generation.} Foot sliding remains well above that of real motion (\tabref{tab:twin_paired}). The final generators have no loss term for foot contact; contact constraints are needed.
\item \textbf{Whole-body motion.} Extending the representation and tokenizer to hands.
\end{enumerate}
